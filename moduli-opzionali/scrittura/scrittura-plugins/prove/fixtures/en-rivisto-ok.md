# Tunguska

At about 7:17 a.m. on June 30, 1908, a bluish light crossed the sky, and farmers saw it from the hills. The blast flattened roughly 2,000 square kilometers of forest. The trees lay in a radial pattern.

According to Leonid Kulik, who reached the site in 1927, the explosion could have happened in the air. Estimates of the energy are uncertain. Mr. Kulik found no crater. Seismic stations in Europe recorded the shock, and in London people reportedly read newspapers outdoors. The U.S. Navy was not involved.
